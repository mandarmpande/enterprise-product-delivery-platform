02 / CLIENT-SIDE PERSPECTIVE

Operating model

How I approach complex programmes.

My focus is not simply activity tracking. It is creating enough structure and transparency for different teams to make decisions at the right time.

1. Make the intake executable

Clarify what is entering delivery, what is missing and what decisions are required before work begins.

2. Make dependencies visible

Connect application, infrastructure, security, data, business and vendor dependencies rather than managing them as isolated workstreams.

3. Create decision transparency

Give stakeholders a shared view of risks, readiness, ownership and escalation rather than relying on status reporting alone.

4. Protect delivery quality

Use readiness criteria, testing discipline and structured defect resolution to reduce avoidable late-cycle surprises.

5. Stay technically credible

Understand enough of the technical landscape to challenge assumptions, coordinate specialists and make informed delivery decisions.

6. Design for adoption

A successful enterprise change is not only technically released; users, governance and operational ownership must be ready.

Selected situations

How I have operated in real enterprise environments.

These are deliberately presented as business situations rather than repeating the bullet points of the resume.

Case 01
Platform modernisation

When an enterprise platform upgrade is also an operating-model change.

An ALM upgrade is rarely just a version change. Application compatibility, Java migration, database and infrastructure changes, security configuration, business testing, release sequencing and stakeholder readiness become interconnected.

  • My role is to connect the technical work with the business readiness needed for the release.
  • The programme requires continuous coordination across multiple specialist domains rather than a single delivery team.
  • The management view therefore needs to show dependencies, risks and readiness, not only completed tasks.
Case 02
Workflow transformation

Turning governance-heavy manual processes into controlled digital workflows.

In enterprise environments, approval processes often sit at the intersection of business policy, auditability, user behaviour and technology. Digitisation therefore changes more than the form users complete.

  • Understand the existing process and the governance intent behind it.
  • Translate the process into structured workflow behaviour and clear ownership.
  • Work with business stakeholders through validation, testing, adoption and handover.
Case 03
Client-side ownership

Moving from delivery partner to platform owner changes the question.

As an implementation partner, the question is often "Can we deliver this?" As a client-side owner, the question becomes broader: "Should we do this, when, with what risk, and what will the organisation need to operate it successfully?"

  • Balance business priorities against technical and operational constraints.
  • Maintain continuity between platform evolution and user needs.
  • Create a common delivery language across business, technology and vendors.
